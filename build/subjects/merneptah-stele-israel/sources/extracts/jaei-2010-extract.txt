In 2001, Manfred Goerg published a new reading of a fragmentary name ring on a topographical pedestal relief in the Berlin Museum (no. 21687). [...] Goerg suggested reading the broken name as an archaic form for "Israel" and argued that it could have been copied during the Nineteenth Dynasty from an earlier list. [...] Two scholars, Bryant Wood and James Hoffmeier, have briefly discussed Goerg's proposal; while the former welcomed it, the latter rejected it on linguistic grounds.
Consequently, we would like to emphasize that the reading of "Israel" on the Berlin pedestal relief is at least possible for two main reasons.
A date later than Ramesses II (for instance, during the reigns of Merenptah or Ramesses III) seems unlikely, however [...]. [On the Berlin relief:] we tentatively ascribe the Berlin pedestal relief to the reign of Ramesses II. Although the reference to "Israel" in association with Ashkelon and Canaan recalls the reference from the reign of Merenptah, a Ramesside date is by no means certain.
