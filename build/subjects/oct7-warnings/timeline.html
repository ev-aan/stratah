People and bodies involved

  • Benjamin Netanyahu: Israel's prime minister; the subject of the claims. Every accusation about what he knew is a claim; he denies receiving any warning from the UAE or Egypt and says the security chiefs did not wake him.
  • Ronen Bar: Then head of the Shin Bet. Says he warned the prime minister in general terms in 2023 and has said he will sue for defamation; the PMO says he knew of 'a real danger' on the night before and did not alert the PM. Accepted responsibility for the Shin Bet's failure on the night (Ynet).
  • Herzi Halevi: Then chief of staff. Said, per the book's account and Ynet, that he was not told of any UAE warning.
  • Tzachi Hanegbi: Then head of the National Security Council. Called the 2023 reports of an Egyptian warning 'utterly fake'; denied to The Atlantic (Sept 2026) any visit or warning.
  • Abbas Kamel: Then head of Egypt's General Intelligence Service. Reported to have visited Israel on 26 Sep 2023 and to have telephoned the prime minister; Egypt has not commented in 2026.
  • Mohamed bin Zayed: UAE president. Reported to have telephoned the prime minister; his government's ministry declined to deny it.
  • Avi Gil: The prime minister's military secretary on 7 Oct 2023; named in the PM's own account as the person who messaged him at 06:29 Israel time.
  • Matanyahu Englman: State Comptroller; his core October 7 audits are suspended by a High Court order.
  • Gadi Eisenkot, Naftali Bennett, Yair Lapid: Opposition figures quoted on the record; their statements are accusations by political rivals.
  • Shlomi Eldar and Ruth Yuval: Journalists; authors of the Haaretz investigation and the book 'Hostages: 843 Days of Abandonment'.
  • Israel's Prime Minister's Office: Issues denials and releases excerpts; a party to the dispute.
